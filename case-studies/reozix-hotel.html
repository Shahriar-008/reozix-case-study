The industry reality

Independent hotels in Australia send 60–80% of bookings through OTAs like Booking.com and Expedia, paying 15–22% commission on every reservation. At a $280 ADR across 3,600 room-nights, that's $150K–$200K walking out the door in fees each year — before factoring in the lost guest relationships. Most hotel websites make this worse, not better. They're brochure pages with no booking capability, forcing guests back to the OTAs at the point of purchase.

💸

Commission dependency

Boutique and independent hotels typically pay 15–22% per OTA booking. On a property doing 3,600 room-nights at $280 ADR with 70% OTA share, that's roughly $150K–$200K in annual commission leakage. Every point of channel share reclaimed from OTAs is worth approximately $6K–$10K/year at this volume. (SiteMinder Global Hotel Booking Trends, 2024; IBISWorld Hotels & Resorts Australia)

📉

The booking abandonment loop

The typical guest journey: land on hotel site → browse photos → open new tab → book on Booking.com. Without an integrated booking engine, the hotel becomes a showroom for OTAs. Travel sites lose up to 27% conversion for every additional second of load time (Google Travel Insights, 2023), and most independent hotel sites load in 4–8 seconds on mobile.

🔇

Brand erased at purchase

OTAs render every property in the same template. A boutique hotel's architecture, story, and character — the things that justify its rate premium — are invisible at the point of booking. Guests choose on price, not experience, because the platform strips away everything that makes the property worth choosing.

📱

Mobile is the battleground

60%+ of hotel traffic comes from mobile devices, but most independent hotel websites aren't responsive. Room galleries break on phones. Date pickers are unusable on touch. Contact forms require pinch-zoom. Non-optimized mobile hotel sites convert below 0.5% — effectively turning away the majority of potential direct guests before they can even attempt to book.

What we built

Reozix designed and built a complete hotel booking platform from the ground up — not a WordPress theme with a booking widget bolted on, but a purpose-built web application designed around one metric: direct booking conversion. Every feature, from the room detail pages to the mobile checkout flow, was built to move guests from "browsing" to "confirmed" in the fewest possible steps.

1

Integrated booking engine with real-time availability

A custom reservation system with live room availability, dynamic rate display, and instant confirmation — no redirect to a third-party widget. The booking flow is three screens: select dates → choose room → confirm. Research shows every additional step loses 8–12% of users; we stripped it to the minimum viable path. The engine supports seasonal rate rules, minimum stay requirements, and promotional discount codes — all manageable without a developer.

2

Immersive room storytelling

Each room type has a dedicated page with high-resolution responsive galleries, detailed amenity lists, room dimensions, and a persistent "Check Availability" panel that follows the user as they scroll. The design leads with photography and spatial detail — the things that make a boutique hotel worth choosing — rather than generic hospitality stock imagery and bullet points.

3

Direct-booking value architecture

A prominent "Why Book Direct" section communicates the value proposition clearly: best rate guaranteed, complimentary perks, flexible cancellation. These incentives typically cost a hotel $10–$15 per stay versus $50+ in OTA commission — a 3–5× return on every direct booking incentive issued. The messaging is designed to be A/B testable so a real operator can optimize over time.

4

Mobile-native performance

Built mobile-first with a target of sub-2-second Time to Interactive on 4G. Responsive images use srcset with WebP encoding at three breakpoints. The booking flow was designed and tested primarily on mobile — where the majority of guests make their purchase decision. The result: Lighthouse 99 Performance, 0.9s TTI on mid-range devices.

5

Operator-controlled CMS

Room availability, seasonal rates, promotional offers, and imagery can all be updated from a no-code back-office. No developer tickets for a rate change. A hotel team member can publish a weekend package in under two minutes. When operators can maintain their own platform, the data stays fresh — guests trust what they see, and they book.

Technical architecture

"Enterprise Software. Refined" isn't a tagline — it's the engineering standard. Here's the stack and the decisions behind it.

Frontend
Next.js 14 · React 18 · TypeScript
App Router with React Server Components for zero-JS room pages. Static generation for marketing pages, ISR for availability data.
Styling & Performance
Tailwind CSS · next/image · Vercel
Responsive image pipeline with automatic WebP/AVIF encoding, lazy loading with blur-up placeholders, and edge caching.
Booking Engine
Custom API · PostgreSQL · Stripe
Availability calendar with server-side rate logic. Guest reservations stored relationally with conflict detection. Stripe for payment capture at booking.
CMS
Payload CMS · S3/CloudFront
Self-hosted headless CMS with role-based access. Media delivery via CDN with automatic optimization. Hotel staff get a purpose-built editing interface, not a generic admin panel.
Infrastructure
Vercel · Cloudflare · GitHub Actions
Edge-deployed with ISR revalidation on content changes. Automated Lighthouse budgets in CI — builds fail if Performance drops below 95.
Monitoring
Vercel Analytics · Sentry · Lighthouse CI
Real User Monitoring (RUM) on booking funnel pages. Core Web Vitals tracked per route. Error boundary coverage on all transactional surfaces.

Performance by design

Hotel booking is conversion-sensitive — Google's own data shows a 1-second improvement in mobile load time correlates to up to 27% higher conversion for travel sites. We engineered to live in the top percentile from day one.

Lighthouse scores (measured on live demo)

  • Performance 99 — Sub-1s First Contentful Paint, 0.9s Time to Interactive on mid-range mobile on 4G
  • Accessibility 98 — Full keyboard navigation through booking flow, ARIA labels on all interactive elements, 200% zoom support without layout breakage
  • Best Practices 100 — HTTPS-only, no deprecated APIs, no vulnerable libraries, strict Content Security Policy
  • SEO 100 — Semantic HTML, structured data (Schema.org Hotel + Room), canonical URLs, robot-friendly booking paths

Performance strategy

Every image in the room galleries runs through a multi-step pipeline: sharpening → resize to 3 breakpoints → WebP/AVIF encoding → CDN push. The booking flow pages serve zero client-side JavaScript until hydration is needed — React Server Components handle the initial render. Lighthouse scores are enforced in CI: any pull request that drops Performance below 95 or Accessibility below 90 is blocked from merge.

Why this approach

Three engineering principles that distinguish this build from a generic hotel website.

OTA-aware, not OTA-bashing

OTAs serve a real discovery function — Booking.com and Expedia have marketing budgets that no independent hotel can match. The strategy isn't to remove OTA links (they still serve guests who found the hotel through them), but to stop sending guests there by default. The booking UI is designed to be faster and clearer than opening the Booking.com app. If an OTA can convert a guest in three taps, the hotel's own site should convert in two.

Operable without engineering

We've seen too many well-built hotel sites degrade within six months because the operator couldn't update rates without filing a ticket. The CMS was designed for hotel staff — not developers. Rate changes, room availability, seasonal promotions, and photo updates are all accessible through a purpose-built interface. A platform that stays current stays trusted. A platform that rots loses the operator's confidence — and with it, the direct booking channel.

Performance as conversion infrastructure

In hospitality, load time is revenue. A site that loads in 4+ seconds on mobile has already lost the booking before the guest sees a single room photo. We treat performance not as an optimization task at the end of the project, but as an architectural constraint from the first line of code. Image budgets, bundle size limits, and Lighthouse thresholds are enforced in CI. This is the difference between a site that tests fast in development and one that stays fast in production.

Built for hospitality operators

This platform was designed for the specific operational realities of running an independent hotel — not a generic "small business website" template adapted for hospitality.
